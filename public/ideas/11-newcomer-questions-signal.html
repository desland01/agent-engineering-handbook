Treat newcomer questions as a signal for onboarding gaps (Theo's dumb-questions practice)

On this page
  1. What was said
  2. How to apply it
  3. When it is useful
  4. Qualification

What was said

Theo describes a practice from his teams: new teammates were asked to ask at least one dumb question per day. Beyond unblocking them, the questions reveal what is and is not working for people discovering the codebase — insight you only get while someone is still a beginner — and inform making the codebase more approachable and preventing regressions.

How to apply it

Make newcomer questions a blameless artifact and triage them periodically: answer the person, then fix the cause — better naming, a missing doc, a lint rule, or a steering-file entry — so the next newcomer (and their agent) never hits it.

When it is useful

Any team onboarding engineers, and equally for agents: an agent's repeated confusion is the same signal.

Qualification

This is a cultural practice Theo describes for his own teams. It is source material, not a workspace rule — no daily question quota is adopted or required here. It depends on psychological safety and does not by itself fix the underlying gaps.
